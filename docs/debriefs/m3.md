## R2 — MLP head vs linear probe (frozen backbones)
| backbone | head | synthetic map_leaf | OpenMIC trained-9 AP |
|---|---|---|---|
| CLAP | linear | 0.446 | 0.656 |
| CLAP | MLP-512 | 0.484 | 0.658 |
| MERT-95M | linear | 0.800 | 0.774 |
| MERT-95M | MLP-512 | 0.824 | 0.787 |

The non-linear head helps in-domain (+0.02–0.04 on synthetic). It barely transfers to
real music (+0.00–0.01). The bottleneck for real audio isn't head capacity; it's the
features and the domain gap.

## C1 — Calibration (F30): which data you calibrate on matters more than how
Temperature scaling fit on held-out data, evaluated on OpenMIC test over trained nodes.

| backbone | calibration | fitted T | ECE | Brier | mAP |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| MERT-95M | none | – | 0.126 | 0.162 | 0.774 |
| MERT-95M | synthetic val, per level | ≈0.8–1.0 | 0.133 | 0.165 | 0.773 |
| MERT-95M | real (OpenMIC train), per level, trained nodes | 3.3 / 3.4 / 1.0 | **0.086** | 0.152 | 0.756 ⚠ |
| MERT-95M | real, **one global T**, trained nodes | 2.94 | 0.096 | **0.150** | **0.774** |
| CLAP | none | – | 0.095 | 0.207 | 0.656 |
| CLAP | real, one global T, trained nodes | 1.95 | 0.100 | 0.202 | 0.656 |

**Findings:**
1. **Synthetic calibration is useless for real use.** On synthetic val the models are
   already calibrated (T ≈ 1), but on real music MERT is about 3× overconfident.
   Calibration has to be fit on real held-out data, which is why §4.4 says "real (not
   synthetic) split". Now measured, not just asserted.
2. **Per-level temperatures don't commute with hierarchy consistency.** The harness
   enforces parent = max(children). Different T per level reorders parents vs children,
   which changed MERT's mAP (0.774 → 0.756). One global temperature is monotone for every
   node, commutes with the max (tested), and keeps mAP. **Decision: global T, fit on a
   real held-out split, over trained nodes only.**
3. **Untrained nodes poison the fit.** Fitting over all OpenMIC classes pushed CLAP's
   level-1 T to the bound (20), because 11 classes were never trained and their scores
   carry no information. Calibrate only nodes the model was trained on.
4. **For CLAP, calibration is roughly neutral** (Brier better, ECE slightly worse). Its
   real-music error is mostly discrimination, not confidence scale.

**Interview Q: "How do you make 0.87 mean 87%?"**
- Fit a post-hoc temperature on a held-out set from the deployment distribution, here
  real recordings.
- Fit only on classes the model actually learned.
- Check it doesn't break other invariants. Per-level temperatures broke hierarchy
  consistency, which a global temperature avoids.

## R5: From scratch (A2), the headline
A 1.22M-param log-mel CNN, random init, trained 40 epochs (~93 s each on MPS) **only on
synthetic clips**. Licence-clean by construction: no third-party weights, and
CC0/CC-BY/MIT sources. The Lakh MIDI caveat still applies to this training set.

| model | params trained | synthetic map_leaf | OpenMIC trained-9 AP |
|---|---|---|---|
| prior | – | 0.219 | 0.538 |
| CLAP probe (Apache-2.0) | 0.02M | 0.446 | 0.656 |
| CLAP LoRA r8 | ~0.3M | 0.467 | 0.663 |
| MERT-95M probe (CC-BY-NC) | 0.03M | 0.800 | 0.774 |
| MERT-95M MLP | 0.4M | 0.824 | 0.787 |
| **from-scratch CNN** | **1.22M** | **0.923** | **0.785** |

- **Scratch − MERT probe on real trained-9 mean AP: +0.011, 95% CI [−0.009, +0.027]**
  (paired artist bootstrap, 500 resamples). Statistically tied.
- **Scratch − CLAP probe on all-20 OpenMIC: CI [+0.013, +0.043]**, a significant win over
  the licence-clean pretrained candidate.
- **Per class on real audio:**
  - scratch is better than MERT on bass (0.544 vs 0.465), organ (0.533 vs 0.391), guitar
    (0.956 vs 0.908), piano and sax;
  - it's worse on drums (0.894 vs 0.961), trumpet (0.665 vs 0.748), synth and cymbals.
- **On untrained classes it's weaker** (0.326 vs 0.364). Expected: no general-audio
  pretraining.

**Why this matters.** The licence-clean path no longer means "settle for a weaker model".
For the classes we render, a small task-specific model trained on perfectly labelled
synthetic data ties a frozen music foundation model on real recordings. Its huge
in-domain lead (0.92 vs 0.80) also shows where the risk is: it's the most specialized to
our renderer, so a measured domain gap (M4) and real eval (MedleyDB) matter most for it.

**Caveat checked:** fine-tuned MERT (LoRA, below) doesn't move either, so the tie holds
against both frozen and adapted MERT at this budget.

## R3: LoRA
- **CLAP LoRA r8** (HTSAT stage 3 + top of stage 2, 3 epochs × ~5 min): trained-9 0.663
  vs probe 0.656; all-20 Δ CI [−0.010, +0.017]. **Not significant.** Adapting CLAP's top
  blocks on synthetic data doesn't move real-music performance.
- **MERT-95M LoRA r8** (layers 8–11 q/k/v/out, 3 epochs, 5 s crops, ~2 h on MPS):
  - synthetic val mAP 0.834 vs probe 0.854; synthetic test map_leaf 0.769 vs 0.800;
  - **real trained-9 0.776 vs probe 0.774, Δ CI [−0.011, +0.015]: no gain.**
  - **Scratch − MERT LoRA: +0.009, CI [−0.010, +0.026]**, still tied.

**Reading R3:**
- Fine-tuning a pretrained backbone *on synthetic audio* doesn't improve real-music
  performance, for either backbone. The adapters fit renderer specifics as easily as
  instrument identity. On the training domain it doesn't even beat the frozen probe at
  this budget (3 epochs, 5 s crops), so undertraining is possible. But the real-music
  numbers say the binding constraint is the domain gap, not adaptation capacity.
- **Decision implication:** a paid full fine-tune (R4) is unlikely to pay off until the
  data side moves (more and more varied renders, domain randomization, real-data
  co-training: M4). GPU money is better spent on bigger from-scratch models trained on
  more data.
